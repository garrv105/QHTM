The Quantum Hardware
Threat Matrix

QHTM is an open, defense-agnostic catalog of adversary techniques against quantum computing hardware and its classical control stack — modeled on MITRE ATT&CK. It covers the surface that fault tolerance will depend on: the QEC syndrome/decoder pipeline and the classical control stack. It is not post-quantum cryptography, which protects classical systems from quantum computers. QHTM maps attacks on the quantum computer itself.

What QHTM is

A shared, versioned vocabulary for quantum-hardware attacks, so researchers, operators, and tool builders can reason about coverage against a named list instead of scattered papers.

  • Defense-agnostic — it names what an adversary does, not how anyone detects it.
  • Honestly maturity-tagged — over-claiming is the failure mode it exists to prevent.
  • Stable, citable technique IDs (e.g. T2.5) that never change meaning.
  • Open under CC BY 4.0 — free to use, cite, and extend with attribution.

What QHTM is not

It is a map of attacks, not a product and not a defense. It is a starting point, and it is deliberately incomplete — the value of a shared map grows as the community corrects and extends it.

  • Not post-quantum cryptography (that's the opposite problem).
  • Not a detection or mitigation method — those are tracked separately and out of scope.
  • Not vendor-specific — the techniques generalize across hardware modalities.
  • Contributions of techniques and maturity evidence are welcome via the repo.

QHTM catalogs adversary techniques for defensive research. It contains no detection or exploitation methods and endorses no product. Maturity tags record whether a technique has been demonstrated on hardware, in simulation, remains theoretical, or is suppressed on current production hardware; they are a safeguard against over-claiming, not a severity ranking. Related weakness and attack-pattern classes have been contributed to MITRE's CWE and CAPEC programs.
